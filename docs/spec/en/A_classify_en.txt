[How to decide]
- Pick domain by the main stage of the worry. If several are mixed, pick the one carrying the most emotion.
- Borders: relationships with people at work and changing jobs → CAR / job-search prep with no job yet → DIR / the spouse themselves → LOV, the spouse's family → FAM / family money trouble where the relationship or burden is central → FAM, managing my own money is central → LIF.
- "Myself" worries (self-esteem and self-judgment, mood swings and how I'm doing inside, meaning and direction in life) all go to DIR with self_focus=true. When career prep (job search, studying, certifications) is central, self_focus=false.
- Judge emotions from the expressions and situation in the text. If a second emotion isn't clear, give only one.
- Pick need by "what does this person want?" If they don't say it directly, infer from the nature of the worry.
  e.g. worn out from never resting → REST / keeps putting off starting → START / at a crossroads, a decision → CHOOSE / wants to get closer, lonely → CONNECT / hard to say no, takes on too much → BOUNDARY / holding onto a breakup, a failure, something already over → LETGO
- Pick qtype by what the user is finally asking, not by the whole text. If they describe the situation at length and end with "Should I …?" or "Should I … or …?", it's yesno; "What are they thinking?" is other_mind; "When will …?" is timing. Description with no question is worry.
  e.g. "…they've left me on read for two days. Should I text one more time?" → qtype yesno, qsub YN-TEXT (when it overlaps with OM-REPLY, go with what the user asks)
- Even for question forms (other_mind, timing, yesno, fortune), pick need by "what this person wants for themselves behind that question."
  e.g. wondering what a situationship feels → wants to get closer: CONNECT, whether to confess is central: CHOOSE /
       does my ex think about me, will we get back together → still holding on: LETGO, has decided to start again: START /
       when will they text → tired of waiting: REST, the relationship is over: LETGO / what a suspected cheater really feels → deciding whether to trust is central: CHOOSE.
  other_mind and timing are mostly locus=other; timing of passing an exam or getting hired is circumstance. fortune gets confidence 0.6 or lower; a love reading defaults to LOV, CONNECT, HOP.
- qsub is "which love situation is this," not the question form. Attach it even to a plain description (worry) if the situation fits (e.g. a long description of being left on read → OM-REPLY).
  Recognize slang: left on read, delivered/unread, double text, dry texting (replies); ghosting, slow fade (ghosting); situationship, breadcrumbing, benching, being kept on the hook, talking stage (defining the relationship); "u up?" texts, zombieing (ex reaching out); orbiting, only watches my stories (social media after ghosting); hot and cold, mind games, avoidant (temperature gap); limerence, falling too fast (my own feelings); monkey-branching (past relationship); soft launch, hard launch (going public).
- Tricky pairs:
  REST↔LETGO: worn out now and wants rest → REST / holding onto a past event, person, or failure → LETGO
  CONNECT↔LETGO: relationship is ongoing or they want a new one → CONNECT / already over but they can't let go → LETGO
  CHOOSE↔START: hasn't decided what to do → CHOOSE / has decided but the first step or the courage to say it is blocked → START
    (e.g. "I don't know if I should break up" → CHOOSE, "I've decided to break up but I can't bring it up" → START)
  BOUNDARY↔REST: other people's demands, taking things on, or control is the cause → BOUNDARY / running on empty is central whatever the cause → REST
  CONNECT↔BOUNDARY: wants to get closer → CONNECT / loses themselves adjusting to others, hard to say no → BOUNDARY
- Judge by the writer's own feelings. If they ask on behalf of a friend or family member, keep the stage as that worry, but pick need and emotions from what the writer shows (worry, hurt, etc.). Don't pick an emotion they deny ("it's not that I'm hurt"), and don't read a hypothetical ("if they ever …") as something that happened. Don't pick by matching words (the word "no" alone isn't BOUNDARY — check whether they take things on or adjust until it hurts).
- For duration, locus and urgency, look only at what the text says directly. Don't guess; if unclear, recent, self, none.
- If the text is meaningless, a joke, or a test sentence, set off_topic=true and fill the rest with the closest values.

[Safety check — if unsure, pick the higher level] (CSO rules, 2026-10-22)
crisis_level:
- "urgent" (stop the cards and show the safety screen): direct statements about death — "I want to die", "kms", "I want to unalive myself", "I'm done living", "I want to go be with (someone who died)", "I wish I'd die first", "I looked up ways to die", "I don't see any reason to keep going", "I won't be here much longer". Self-harm acts ("I've been cutting", "I hurt myself on purpose", "I have a habit of hurting myself"). Preparing to say goodbye to the world ("I wrote letters to my family", "I've been saving up pills", "I'm giving my things away"). It is the same after a breakup, job loss, or exam. Hearing voices that aren't there, being certain they're being watched, and taking a lot of pills at once are also urgent.
  A partner who keeps you from leaving by saying "If you break up with me, I'll kill myself" is not urgent — it is violence (a threat / coercion).
- "watch" (show the results as usual and add one support line on the last page): passive statements — "I wish I could disappear", "what's the point of living", "I don't know why I'm alive", "I'd be better off not living", "I wish I could just vanish", "I hope I don't wake up tomorrow", "I want to sleep forever", "everyone would be better off without me" (feeling like a burden), "I just want it all to end", "I wish tomorrow wouldn't come". Not eating or not sleeping for a month or more, can't get through without drinking, staying in their room and not going out, hopeless certainty like "it will never change, ever", extreme fasting or eating and throwing up.
- "none": everything else. Not eating or no appetite for less than a month after a breakup or exam ("It's been four days since we broke up and I've barely eaten"), "I'm going to end it" meaning ending a relationship or finishing an exam or a job, how money is handled between spouses or partners (it may be an agreement between them), and figures of speech ("I'm starving to death", "I literally died laughing", "I was so embarrassed I wanted to disappear lol", "the thing I want to end is this relationship", "this exam is killing me").
  But if a figure of speech comes with signs like not eating for more than a month or staying in their room, it is watch.
violence (true/false — turns off the direction calculation and shows counseling-center and police information): only concrete acts — hitting, pushing, or choking someone; threats ("I'll kill you", "I'll post your photos", "you'll regret it", or keeping you from leaving with "If you break up with me, I'll kill myself"); checking their phone or demanding passwords ("show me all your messages"); forcing location sharing; keeping them from seeing people; taking their money; stalking (shows up at home or work when they try to break up, keeps contacting them after they change numbers). The same applies in families and workplaces. Mutually agreed password sharing with no pressure is false. Money-management styles alone (setting a living allowance, checking receipts, managing the bank account) are false.
fear (true/false — show only the info box; the direction is still calculated): throwing things, hitting the wall or desk, "I'm scared of them", "I walk on eggshells and can't say anything". "I'm scared they'll leave" (fear of losing them) or just saying they blocked someone is false.
